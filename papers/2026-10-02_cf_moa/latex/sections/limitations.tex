\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Development exposure and confirmation.} The full v5 benchmark was already exposed
and contributed to baseline repair, terminal-head development, and later composition
selection. Current bootstrap intervals do not remove that exposure. Frozen component
evaluations on ASCENT, CUPCase, constructed rule cases, and new physiological profiles
were completed and retain their stated narrow scope. ASCENT's 47-record check combines
source-training and source-test records and was subsequently exposed to further
development; only its initial frozen comparison retains that evaluation status.
A planned new-family evaluation
of the final CF-MoA composition and a second independently generated F pool were
deferred and have not been executed. The final composition therefore lacks independent
confirmation and across-pool replication; this does not reclassify the completed
component checks as unexecuted.

\textbf{Scope and scoring.} The sources differ in task semantics, patient realism,
perturbation construction, and reference quality. R labels overlap, some groups are small,
and the 13-unit R4 clinical subset has especially limited coverage. Classification follows
the project's documented target-level review rules; no clinician inter-annotator agreement
estimate is reported. Canonical diagnostic matching can penalize clinically related
wording, while source expected answers do not provide a new clinical adjudication. An
empty list of newly established data defects in the final closeout is not evidence that
all items and labels have undergone a complete expert medical review.

\textbf{Attribution and model comparison.} Seven configurations share two backbones and
six method families, and their upstream resources and budgets differ. Rule-head results
reuse backbone-specific facts with seven initial-answer sets, while diagnostic
re-decision uses each configuration's actual context. Neither procedure establishes
seven independent data replications or seven new complete upstream runs. B combines
already developed components with additional resources and inference. Specified local
comparisons, such as fixed-fact rule ablations and C3 versus C2, isolate narrower design
changes; single-pass and repeated catalog decisions have different call budgets. M4 and M5
differ in output protocol as well as model. Their disagreement in C3's effect is
configuration dependence; a pure backbone explanation requires matched-protocol
experiments. Candidate-trigger membership also differs because each model generates its
own pool.

\textbf{Component coverage.} The rule head covers a limited public rule bank and fixed
question-scope patterns, and can inherit errors through UNKNOWN states. R2's answer
imbalance favors the constant ``none'' control. The diagnostic catalog is finite, and
the small external panels do not establish stable calibration gains. R4's executor
requires a complete supplied model; the later R5 learning candidates were not adopted.
The seven-system R1--R3 head table does not establish seven-system R4/R5 optimization.

\textbf{Clinical applicability.} This is an offline benchmark study, not a prospective
patient study, treatment trial, or deployment evaluation. Rule coverage is limited, and
the A4 physiological results concern a supplied mathematical model. Neither exact-match
improvements nor successful model execution establish clinical safety or correctness
outside those contracts.

\textbf{Computation and reproducibility.} Some original-system costs and historical
timings are unknown. Shared-call attribution describes the measured components rather than
complete end-to-end resource use. The figure package reproduces reported summaries and can
check them against the released compressed result records; it does not rerun the full
language-model experiment. Original input data, licenses, model access, retrieval indices,
and environment-specific paths remain separate prerequisites for fresh inference.
