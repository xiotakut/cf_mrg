\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CF-MoA provides an operation-based account of support updating and candidate decisions in
medical question answering. Across two MedRAG configurations on the complete exposed v5
benchmark, the adopted core improves the native-unit mean substantially.
Rationale-conditioned verification of its existing candidates adds a smaller,
configuration-dependent effect: negative on M4 and positive on M5. The six-arm comparison,
operation ablations, paired outcomes, and computation ledger show why system gains and
verification gains require separate evidence. Independent confirmation and candidate-pool
replication remain the next empirical steps before stronger generalization claims can be
made.
