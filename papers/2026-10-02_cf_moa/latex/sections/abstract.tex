\begin{abstract}
Medical question answering requires deciding when changed information adds support,
invalidates a premise, alters candidate rankings, or leaves a judgment intact. We evaluate
seven system configurations spanning six baseline families on a frozen benchmark of 13,905
inputs per configuration and 6,104 non-reference native units from 11 sources. Error
analysis motivates two reusable terminal components: question-scoped rule execution and
original-model re-decision within a public diagnostic catalog. These components improve
every configuration on the complete R1, R2, and R3 development strata, containing 2,866,
79, and 733 units. Gains range from 14.83--19.12, 16.46--29.11, and 18.69--35.20
percentage points, respectively. The strata overlap; they are not independent replications.
Matched controls show substantial benefits from the catalog interface, variable additional
effects of voting and calibration, and a prescribing head that preserves some remaining
support but trails a same-coverage constant-answer control in aggregate accuracy. We then
study the CF-MoA composition and additional candidate verification in a six-arm experiment
on the two MedRAG configurations. Its adopted core improves the full native-unit mean by
10.09 and 12.01 points. Rationale-conditioned verification changes that mean by $-1.81$
and $+0.93$ points, with 95\% paired family intervals of [$-2.53$, $-1.08$] and
[$+0.30$, $+1.56$]. The study establishes consistent terminal-component gains across seven
development configurations while separating those gains from the narrower, mixed effect
of additional verification. Small external component evaluations delimit transfer;
independent confirmation of the final composition remains unexecuted.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} medical question answering; counterfactual evaluation; support
updating; candidate verification; retrieval-augmented generation
