\begin{abstract}
Changes to a medical question can introduce support, invalidate a decision premise, alter
candidate rankings, or leave the correct answer unchanged. Evaluating these behaviors
requires separating the benefits of a decision system from the value of additional
verification. We study CF-MoA, an operation-based system that routes visible task inputs
to established support recomputation, diagnostic candidate comparison, conditional model
execution, or repeated native-answer readout. We then evaluate rationale-conditioned
candidate verification, which selects among complete answers already produced by the
readout component. A six-arm evaluation covers two MedRAG configurations and 13,905 inputs
per configuration, scored as 6,104 non-reference native units from 11 sources. The adopted
core improves the native-unit mean over the original systems by 10.09 and 12.01 percentage
points. Additional rationale-conditioned verification changes that mean by \ensuremath{-}1.81 points on
the Llama configuration and +0.93 points on the Qwen configuration, with corresponding
95\% paired family bootstrap intervals of [\ensuremath{-}2.53, \ensuremath{-}1.08] and [+0.30, +1.56].
Rationale inclusion improves point estimates relative to candidate-only verification, but
does not produce consistent gains over the core. Source-level results, paired correctness,
operation ablations, and attributed request costs expose this distinction. All results
come from an already exposed development benchmark; independent confirmation and a second
candidate-generation replicate remain unexecuted. The findings support operation-level
evaluation while limiting claims of a general benefit from additional candidate
verification.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} medical question answering; counterfactual evaluation; support
updating; candidate verification; retrieval-augmented generation
